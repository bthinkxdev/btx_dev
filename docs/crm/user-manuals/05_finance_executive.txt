--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2. YOUR DASHBOARD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Dashboards -> Finance: receivables ageing, collections today/period, GST/
TDS position, pending verifications. Set the date range (Today / This week

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3. INVOICES — Money -> Invoices
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 Status lifecycle
  Draft -> Pending approval -> Issued -> Partially paid -> Paid
  (or Credit noted / Cancelled / Written off at various points)
  INV_OPEN (has a balance to collect) = Issued, Partially paid.

3.2 Creating and issuing an invoice
  1) Money -> Invoices -> Add invoice (standalone) or from a Won
     quotation/contract (keeps the line items and client linked).
  2) Check line items, tax (GST), place of supply and the client's GSTIN/
     customer type are correct — these drive the tax calculation and your
     GST reports. Preview totals before saving.
  3) Save as Draft. If it needs approval (above policy limit, or a manual
     override), submit — it moves to Pending approval in the approver's
     queue.
  4) "Issue" — this locks the invoice number from the configured numbering
     series and makes it live/collectable; it cannot be freely edited after
     this (use a Credit note for corrections instead, see 3.4).
  5) "Send" delivers the PDF to the client; "Remind" (single, or bulk via
     Invoices -> Remind on the list) nudges them for payment.
  Must do: never back-date or renumber an issued invoice; raise a credit
  note for any correction once issued.

3.3 Collecting against an invoice
  - Record a payment directly (see section 4), or create a Payment link
    from the invoice.
  - "Apply advance" pulls an existing advance/credit balance on the client
    onto this invoice.
  - "Promise to pay" logs a client's payment promise with a date, useful
    for the receivables follow-up queue.
  - "Dispute" / "Resolve dispute" if the client contests the amount —
    track the conversation on the invoice until resolved.

3.4 Corrections and write-offs
  - "Credit" raises a Credit note against the invoice (reason: Return /
    Cancellation / Discount after sale / other) — goes Draft -> Pending
    approval -> Issued, same numbering-series discipline as invoices.
  - "Write off" only for genuinely uncollectable balances, with a reason —
    this is a finance decision, document it clearly; it affects receivables
    reporting and the P&L.
  - "Cancel" an invoice only while still unpaid and before it should have
    counted as revenue — needs a reason and, above your limit, approval.

3.5 Credit notes — Money -> Credit notes
  List and PDF of every credit note issued; same status lifecycle pattern
  as invoices (Draft -> Pending approval -> Issued, or Cancelled).

3.6 Receivables — Money -> Receivables
  Ageing view of every outstanding balance (current / 30 / 60 / 90+ days),
  by client. This is your daily collections work list — sort by days
  overdue, call/email the oldest first, log a "Promise to pay" where you
  get one.

3.7 Advances — Money -> Advances
  Client advance balances not yet applied to an invoice. Apply them from
  the invoice ("Apply advance") rather than leaving them sitting idle.

3.8 TDS receivable — Money -> TDS receivable
  Tracks TDS the client deducted on your invoices: Expected -> Certificate
  received -> Claimed in return. Mark certificates received as you get
  Form 16A from clients; this must reconcile with your tax filings.

3.9 Client statements — open from a Client's detail page -> Statement
  A full running account statement for one client (every invoice, credit
  note, payment) — send this when a client asks "what do I owe you".

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4. PAYMENTS — Money -> Payments
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1 Status lifecycle
  Pending verification -> Verified (or Rejected) -> ... -> Partially
  refunded / Refunded. Only Verified (and refund states) count toward
  collected revenue.

4.2 Recording a payment
  Payments -> Record payment: pick client/invoice, amount, method (UPI,
  Card, Netbanking, Wallet, Bank transfer, Cheque, Cash, Other), reference
  number, date, attach proof (screenshot/bank entry) if manual. Gateway
  (UPI/Card/Netbanking/Wallet) payments usually arrive already verified via
  the webhook; manual methods start as Pending verification.

4.3 Verifying payments — Payments -> Verify queue
  This is your core daily task. For each pending payment:
  1) Open it, check the proof attached against the amount and client.
  2) "Verify" if it checks out — it becomes Verified and allocates against
     the invoice/link it was recorded for.
  3) "Reject" (with a reason) if it doesn't check out — the submitter is
     notified to correct and resubmit.
  4) "Accept for review" if you need a second pair of eyes before a final
     call on a borderline case.
  Must do: verify or reject every item daily — nothing collected counts
  anywhere in reporting until it is Verified.

4.4 Allocating a payment — Payments -> Allocation, or from the payment
  If a payment wasn't tied to one specific invoice (e.g. a lump sum against
  several), use "Allocate" to split it across the right invoices/links.
  "Reverse allocation" undoes a wrong split (with a reason) so you can
  redo it correctly.

4.5 Refunds — Money -> Refunds
  Requested -> Approved -> (processed) / Rejected. Review the request
  against the original payment, "Approve" + "Refund" to execute it (or
  "Reject" with a reason). This also triggers the matching credit note/
  invoice adjustment where relevant.

4.6 Disputes (chargebacks) — tracked per payment
  Open -> Evidence submitted -> Won / Lost. Attach your evidence (invoice,
  delivery proof, communication) before the gateway's deadline; track
  status here rather than only in the payment gateway's own portal.

4.7 Gateway events — Payments -> Gateway events
  Raw webhook events from the payment gateway (Received -> Processed /
  Duplicate / ...). Use this to debug "the client paid but nothing
  updated" — find the event, check its status, and "Reprocess" it if it's
  stuck, or "Ignore" if it's confirmed irrelevant/duplicate.

4.8 Settlements — Money -> Settlements
  Gateway settlement batches (money actually landing in your bank). Match
  these against verified payments during bank reconciliation (section 6).

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5. PAYMENT LINKS — Money -> Collections -> Payment links
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
You can create, approve (within policy) and cancel payment links company-
wide. Same lifecycle and fields as described in the Sales manuals (02/03,
section 7) — your extra responsibility is approving links above a sales
rep's own limit, and investigating any link stuck "Active" with no
matching Gateway event.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6. BANKING — Money -> Banking
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
6.1 Bank accounts — Banking -> Accounts: your company's bank accounts on
  file; add a new one if the company opens one.
6.2 Statement import and reconciliation — Banking -> Reconciliation
  1) Import a bank statement file (CSV/format your bank exports).
  2) Each transaction line comes in Unmatched; the system suggests matches
     against payments/expenses (Suggested) — confirm correct suggestions to
     Matched, or match manually.
  3) Once every line for the period is matched (or deliberately left
     unmatched with a note), "Finalise" the reconciliation — this locks it.
  4) "Undo" is available shortly after finalising if you catch a mistake;
     after that, raise a correcting entry instead.
  Open Banking -> Reconciliation -> a past reconciliation's "Report" to see
  the finalised record for an audit or month-end review.
Must do: reconcile every statement import before month-end close; don't
  let unmatched lines pile up across periods.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7. EXPENSES AND VENDORS — Money -> Expenses / Vendors
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
7.1 Status lifecycle
  Draft -> Submitted -> Approved (or Rejected / Correction requested) ->
  Partially paid -> Paid (or Reversed)

7.2 Approving expenses — Expenses -> Approvals
  Queue of Submitted expenses waiting on you. Open each, check the
  category, amount and attached receipt:
  - "Approve" if valid — it becomes payable.
  - "Reject" (reason required) if it shouldn't be paid.
  - "Request correction" if the claim is fixable (wrong category, missing
    receipt) — it goes back to the submitter to fix and resubmit.
  Must do: never approve an expense with no receipt attached except where
  company policy explicitly allows it (e.g. small per-diems) — check
  Settings -> Policy if unsure.

7.3 Paying expenses
  Once Approved, "Pay" records the payout (bank transfer/cheque/cash),
  moving it to Partially paid/Paid. "Allocate" splits a payout across
  multiple approved expenses if you pay several at once. "Reverse" undoes
  an incorrect payment entry (with a reason).

7.4 Recurring expenses — Expenses -> Recurring
  Rent, subscriptions, retainers that repeat on a schedule. Active / Paused
  / Ended. Review monthly — pause anything no longer needed rather than
  letting it keep generating.

7.5 TDS payable — Expenses -> TDS payable
  TDS you owe to deduct/deposit on vendor bills, by section (194C
  Contractors, 194J Professional/technical, etc.). Reconcile this against
  your TDS challans each filing period.

7.6 Vendors — Money -> Vendors
  Vendor master: Active / On hold / Blocked. Open a vendor to see every
  expense/bill against them and their running balance. Set a vendor
  On hold/Blocked to stop new bills being raised against them without
  deleting their history.

11. APPROVALS — YOUR INBOX
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Expense approvals, invoice/credit-note approvals above policy limits, and
refund approvals land here. Clear "Mine" daily — a stuck approval blocks
payroll-linked reimbursements, vendor payments and client collections
alike.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
12. YOUR DAILY / WEEKLY / MONTHLY CHECKLIST
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Daily:
  [ ] Payments -> Verify queue — clear to zero.
  [ ] Expenses -> Approvals — clear to zero.
  [ ] Approvals -> Mine — clear to zero.
  [ ] Receivables — chase anything newly overdue.
Weekly:
  [ ] Banking -> Reconciliation — import and match the latest statement.
  [ ] Gateway events — any stuck/Failed entries to investigate.
  [ ] Refunds — any pending beyond a couple of days.
Monthly:
  [ ] TDS receivable / TDS payable reconciled against filings.
  [ ] Recurring expenses reviewed (pause/end what's no longer needed).
  [ ] Receivables ageing report sent to Sales Manager/Owner for follow-up
      on anything 60+ days.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
13. TROUBLESHOOTING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
"Client says they paid but it's not showing" -> check Payments -> Gateway
  events for the matching webhook; "Reprocess" if found but stuck.
"Can't edit an issued invoice" -> correct, by design — issued invoices are
  locked; raise a Credit note for any correction.
"Payment stuck in Pending verification" -> that's your queue; verify or
  reject it — it isn't collected revenue until you act.
"Reconciliation won't finalise" -> every line must be Matched or
  deliberately left with a note; check for stray Unmatched/Suggested rows.
"I can't post/edit a journal entry" -> correct — that's the Accountant's
  permission, not yours; raise it with them.
